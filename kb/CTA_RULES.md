# CTA_RULES

Version: 1.0

---

# 1. Purpose

This knowledge base guides Gemini AI in generating clear, relevant, and platform-appropriate calls to action.

The CTA must support the content objective without making the copy feel forced, repetitive, or overly promotional.

---

# 2. Scope

The CTA Rules define:

- CTA objective
- CTA type
- CTA placement
- CTA wording
- CTA intensity
- Platform adaptation
- Affiliate CTA behavior
- Engagement CTA behavior

The CTA Rules do not define:

- Brand Voice
- Caption structure
- SEO strategy
- Compliance rules
- Storyboards
- Voice-over scripts
- T2I prompts
- I2V prompts

---

# 3. Core Principles

## 3.1 One Primary Action

Each output must prioritize one clear action.

Do not combine multiple competing actions in one CTA.

## 3.2 Contextual Relevance

The CTA must directly relate to:

- The content
- The audience
- The product
- The platform
- The communication objective

## 3.3 Natural Delivery

The CTA must feel like a natural continuation of the content.

It must not sound disconnected, aggressive, or overly commercial.

## 3.4 Value Before Action

Deliver useful information, relevance, or product value before asking the audience to act.

## 3.5 Low Friction

Use simple wording that makes the requested action easy to understand.

---

# 4. CTA Categories

## 4.1 Product Discovery CTA

Use when the objective is to encourage viewers to check an affiliate product.

Action examples:

- Check the product in the cart.
- See the product details.
- Find the product used in this video.
- Check the tool used for this method.

Use only when the product is relevant to the content.

---

## 4.2 Save CTA

Use when the content contains:

- Recipes
- Tutorials
- Step-by-step methods
- Checklists
- Practical tips
- Reference information

Action examples:

- Save this for later.
- Save the recipe so you can try it.
- Keep this method for your next meal prep.
- Save this tip before you forget it.

---

## 4.3 Share CTA

Use when the content is useful to a specific person or group.

Action examples:

- Share this with someone who needs it.
- Send this to your meal-prep partner.
- Share this with someone who loves simple recipes.

Do not use vague wording such as “share this everywhere.”

---

## 4.4 Comment CTA

Use when the content naturally supports a response.

Action examples:

- Which version would you choose?
- Have you tried this method?
- What ingredient would you add?
- Which result do you prefer?

The question must be:

- Easy to answer
- Relevant to the content
- Specific
- Free from engagement bait

---

## 4.5 Follow CTA

Use when the content is part of a repeatable series or clear topic category.

Action examples:

- Follow for more practical recipes.
- Follow for more simple meal-prep ideas.
- Follow for more product-use demonstrations.

Do not use a follow CTA in every output.

---

## 4.6 Action CTA

Use when the audience can directly apply the content.

Action examples:

- Try this method at home.
- Use this step for your next batch.
- Test this version and compare the result.
- Apply this tip the next time you cook.

---

# 5. CTA Selection Rules

Choose the CTA based on the primary content objective.

If the objective is affiliate conversion:

- Use a Product Discovery CTA.

If the objective is education:

- Use a Save CTA or Action CTA.

If the objective is engagement:

- Use a Comment CTA.

If the objective is reach:

- Use a Share CTA when naturally relevant.

If the objective is audience growth:

- Use a Follow CTA only when the content represents an ongoing topic or series.

If multiple objectives are present:

- Select one primary CTA.
- Use one secondary CTA only when the platform and format allow it.
- The secondary CTA must not compete with the primary CTA.

---

# 6. Affiliate CTA Rules

Affiliate CTAs must:

- Connect the product to the demonstrated use case.
- Mention the product only after its relevance is clear.
- Use neutral and natural wording.
- Avoid creating false urgency.
- Avoid unsupported superiority claims.
- Avoid implying guaranteed results.
- Avoid pressuring the audience.

Preferred approach:

1. Show or explain the product's role.
2. State why it is relevant.
3. Invite the audience to check it.

Preferred CTA direction:

- Check the product used in the video.
- The tool used here is available in the cart.
- See the product details in the cart.
- Check the ingredient used for this recipe.

Avoid:

- Buy now before it is too late.
- You must have this.
- This is guaranteed to work.
- Everyone needs this product.
- The best product on the market.
- Click immediately.
- Do not miss out.

---

# 7. Engagement CTA Rules

Engagement CTAs must encourage genuine interaction.

Use:

- Relevant questions
- Preference-based questions
- Experience-based questions
- Simple comparison questions

Good examples:

- Which texture do you prefer?
- Have you tried storing it this way?
- What would you add to this recipe?
- Which method works better for you?

Avoid:

- Comment “YES” if you agree.
- Type a number to claim this.
- Comment anything for the link.
- Like and share to receive a benefit.
- Tag five people now.
- Comment repeatedly.

---

# 8. CTA Placement Rules

## Short-Form Captions

Place the CTA near the end of the caption.

## Long-Form Captions

Place the CTA after the main value and product context.

## Voice-Over

Place the CTA in the closing segment unless the storyboard requires an earlier action.

## YouTube Description

Place the primary CTA after the content summary or product explanation.

## On-Screen Text

Keep the CTA short and readable.

Do not place the CTA before the audience understands the content value.

---

# 9. Platform Rules

## 9.1 TikTok

Use:

- One short CTA
- Direct and conversational wording
- Product discovery, comment, save, or follow actions

Recommended length:

- 3 to 12 words

Examples:

- Check the product in the cart.
- Save this recipe for later.
- Which version would you try?
- Follow for more simple recipes.

---

## 9.2 Facebook

Use:

- One primary CTA
- One optional secondary CTA
- Slightly more explanatory wording

Examples:

- Save this method so you can try it later.
- Share this with someone who often prepares weekly meals.
- The product used here is available in the product link.

---

## 9.3 Instagram

Prioritize:

- Save
- Share
- Comment
- Product discovery

Examples:

- Save this for your next breakfast.
- Share this with someone who loves practical recipes.
- Which topping would you choose?
- Check the product used in this video.

---

## 9.4 YouTube

Use:

- A clear action related to the video
- Product discovery when relevant
- Follow or subscribe only when appropriate

Examples:

- Check the product details in the description.
- Try the method and share your result.
- Subscribe for more practical recipes.
- Save this video for your next meal prep.

---

# 10. CTA Intensity Levels

## Level 1 — Soft

Use for educational, inspirational, or awareness content.

Examples:

- Save this for later.
- You can try this method at home.
- The product used here is available in the cart.

## Level 2 — Direct

Use for affiliate, tutorial, or product demonstration content.

Examples:

- Check the product used in this video.
- See the product details in the cart.
- Try this tool for your next batch.

## Level 3 — Strong

Use only when the campaign brief explicitly requires a stronger commercial CTA.

The CTA must remain:

- Truthful
- Non-coercive
- Free from false urgency
- Consistent with compliance rules

Examples:

- Check the product while it is currently available.
- See the current product offer in the cart.

Do not invent scarcity, deadlines, discounts, or stock conditions.

---

# 11. Prohibited CTA Patterns

Never use CTAs that:

- Create false urgency
- Invent scarcity
- Promise guaranteed outcomes
- Use fear or intimidation
- Shame the audience
- Manipulate engagement
- Request unrelated actions
- Contain unsupported promotional claims
- Use excessive capitalization
- Use excessive punctuation
- Repeat the same action multiple times
- Ask for too many actions at once

Avoid patterns such as:

- BUY NOW!!!
- CLICK BEFORE IT IS GONE!!!
- You will regret missing this.
- Only smart people use this.
- This guarantees success.
- Like, comment, follow, save, and share now.
- Tag everyone you know.
- Comment to unlock the secret.

---

# 12. Decision Tree

Use the following sequence:

1. Identify the primary content objective.
2. Identify the most relevant audience action.
3. Select one CTA category.
4. Match the CTA to the platform.
5. Match the CTA intensity to the content.
6. Confirm that the CTA follows the Brand Voice.
7. Confirm that the CTA is supported by the content.
8. Remove competing or unnecessary actions.
9. Verify that the CTA does not contain prohibited patterns.

---

# 13. Output Specification

Each CTA output must include:

- `cta_type`
- `cta_objective`
- `cta_text`
- `cta_intensity`
- `cta_placement`

Allowed `cta_type` values:

- `product_discovery`
- `save`
- `share`
- `comment`
- `follow`
- `action`

Allowed `cta_intensity` values:

- `soft`
- `direct`
- `strong`

Example:

```json
{
  "cta_type": "product_discovery",
  "cta_objective": "affiliate_conversion",
  "cta_text": "Check the product used in this video.",
  "cta_intensity": "direct",
  "cta_placement": "caption_closing"
}
```

Every CTA must be:

- Clear
- Relevant
- Natural
- Concise
- Platform-appropriate
- Consistent with the Brand Voice
- Supported by the content
- Ready for publication
